--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. PROJECT DATA OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Purpose of Dataset A
  Dataset A is the raw daily adjusted-close price history for the 100 official
  assets of the M6 forecasting competition. It is the single upstream source
  from which all later work (return calculation, rolling-origin evaluation
  windows, model inference, quintile conversion, RPS scoring) will be derived.
  Keeping it raw — no filling, no calendar alignment, no return computation —
  ensures every downstream transformation is explicit, reproducible and
  auditable.

Official asset universe
  The universe is defined by the `symbol` column of the official M6 file
  supplied with the project (Data/assets_m6.csv; also copied unmodified to
  data/raw/m6_official/assets_m6.csv for validation use). It contains exactly
  100 unique symbols — 50 US stocks and 50 ETFs (including six London-listed
  iShares ETFs with `.L` suffixes). The unique symbols are extracted
  programmatically in first-occurrence order; the list is never hardcoded or
  alphabetised, so the official ordering is preserved end-to-end.

Required date range
  2020-01-01 through 2023-02-03 inclusive. This spans a pre-competition
  history buffer plus the M6 competition period. The buffer exists so that
  each forecast origin can look back over a long context window (up to 512
  daily observations) without reaching before the data start.

Relationship to later rolling-origin evaluation
  Later stages will evaluate forecasts on a rolling-origin basis across the
  competition period. Each origin requires a trailing window of historical
  returns; the earliest origin (around 2022-03-04) therefore needs roughly
  513 prior price observations (513 prices -> 512 returns). Dataset A is
  validated against that requirement but deliberately does not construct the
  windows itself.

B. STAGE 1 — INITIAL YAHOO FINANCE ACQUISITION (executed 2026-07-17)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Source and tooling
  Yahoo Finance via the `yfinance` Python package (v1.5.1), Python 3.10.11,
  pandas 2.2.2. Script: scripts/download_dataset_a.py.

Download parameters
  start=2020-01-01, end=2023-02-04 (yfinance end is exclusive, so the last
  included date is 2023-02-03), interval=1d, auto_adjust=False, actions=False,

  approximate total returns, matching how the M6 competition treated prices.

  - 97 of 100 assets downloaded successfully.
  - 3 assets failed entirely: DRE, RE, WRK. Yahoo Finance purges delisted
    tickers, and all three had left the market after the competition window
    (DRE acquired by Prologis, Oct 2022; RE renamed to EG, Jul 2023; WRK
    merged into Smurfit WestRock, Jul 2024). They were retained as all-null
    columns — never silently dropped — and the dataset was explicitly
    reported as structurally valid but NOT data complete.
  - CARR (first observation 2020-03-19) and OGN (2021-06-03) have genuinely
    shorter histories because they are spin-offs (from United Technologies

    values at the change date.

Raw-data rules followed (and why no filling occurred)
  No weekend rows were added, no shared business-day calendar was built, no
  forward/backward filling, interpolation, zero-substitution, return
  calculation or standardisation was performed, and the official M6 file was
  not used to patch Yahoo gaps. US and London exchanges observe different
  holidays, so some dates are legitimately missing for some assets; filling
  them here would silently bake a modelling decision into the "raw" layer and
  make later methodological choices unauditable. All such decisions are
  deferred to Stage 2 where they can be documented explicitly.

C. STAGE 1B — EODHD REPAIR OF DRE, RE AND WRK (executed 2026-07-17)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why EODHD
  EODHD retains delisted securities' histories (Yahoo does not), and the
  official M6 organisers used EODHD during the competition, so it is the most
  defensible provider for reconstructing the three missing series. An active
  subscription was verified programmatically before download (token valid,
  monthly plan, 100,000 requests/day, historical EOD endpoint accessible with
  the `adjusted_close` field). The API token lives only in the local env file
  (git-ignored, never tracked) and appears in no code, log, report or output.

Script
  scripts/repair_dataset_a_eodhd.py

Endpoint and parameters (token excluded)
  GET https://eodhd.com/api/eod/{IDENTIFIER}
      from=2020-01-01, to=2023-02-03 (inclusive), period=d, order=a, fmt=json
  Field extracted: `adjusted_close` only. `close`/`open`, acquirer series and
  official M6 values were never substituted.

Identifier discovery
  Candidates were discovered programmatically from three sources — the
  current US exchange symbol list (51,535 rows), the delisted US list
  (58,583 rows) and the EODHD search endpoint — queried with both tickers
  and company names (DRE / Duke Realty Corporation; RE / Everest Re Group;
  EG / Everest Group; WRK / WestRock Company). Candidates were never accepted
  on ticker text alone; company identity, coverage and return agreement with
  the official M6 series all had to hold.

Identifiers tested and outcomes
  DRE:
    DRE.US    (Duke Realty Corporation, delisted list) — ACCEPTED.
              694 rows, 2020-01-02 .. 2022-10-03. Overlap vs official DRE
              (restricted to <= 2022-10-03, see below): 170 observations,
              return correlation 1.000000, median |dR| 4e-06, max 1.9e-05,
              no major disagreements, no price-ratio jumps.
    DRE-PL.US (a Duke Realty preferred line) — rejected: empty response.
  RE:
    EG.US     (Everest Group Ltd, active list) — ACCEPTED. EODHD stores one
              continuous series under the current ticker: 779 rows,
              2020-01-02 .. 2023-02-03, fully covering the pre-rename RE era.
              Overlap vs official RE: 255 observations, correlation 1.000000,
              median |dR| 1e-06, max 4e-06. No separate RE.US or "RE_old"
              identifier exists in EODHD's current or delisted US lists.
    (No other Everest candidates were returned by the lists or search.)
  WRK:
    WRK.US    (WestRock Co, delisted list) — ACCEPTED. 779 rows,
              2020-01-02 .. 2023-02-03. Overlap vs official WRK: 255
              observations, correlation 1.000000, median |dR| 7e-06,
              max 2.6e-05.
    SW.US     (Smurfit WestRock plc) — rejected: return correlation 0.297,
              median |dR| 0.019. Confirms SW is NOT a continuation of the
              original WestRock security and must not be substituted.
    WEST.US / WESTW.US (Westrock Coffee — unrelated company) — rejected:
              wrong identity, late starts, near-zero correlations.

Validation method
  For each candidate: align with the official M6 price series by date over
  the overlapping window (2022-01-31 onward), compute daily simple returns
  independently on both aligned series, and compare: overlap count, first and
  last overlap dates, return correlation, median and maximum absolute return
  difference, dates with |dR| > 0.01, and day-on-day jumps (> 1%) in the
  price-level ratio (a corporate-action discontinuity screen). Acceptance
  thresholds: >= 60 overlapping observations, correlation >= 0.99, median
  |dR| <= 0.002, history starting by 2020-01-10 and reaching the required end
  date, company identity confirmed by name. Price LEVELS were compared only
  diagnostically: providers may adjust from different anchor dates, so levels
  can differ while the security is identical; return agreement is the
  identity test. No recovered series was rescaled to force agreement.

Special handling decisions
  DRE: the official M6 file freezes DRE's price at 48.20 from 2022-09-30
    onward (the competition's zero-return treatment after the Prologis
    acquisition). The official series was therefore truncated at 2022-10-03
    for validation, and only genuine traded observations (through 2022-10-03,
    DRE's final trading day) were retained in the repaired dataset. Dates
    after that remain missing at this stage. PLD was not used.
  RE/EG: RE remains the canonical Dataset A column name (the research
    evaluation period ends before the July 2023 rename); the data provider
    identifier was EG.US and is documented as such. No EG column exists.
  WRK: only the original WestRock security (delisted WRK.US) was accepted.

Data provenance
  The unchanged JSON records for the three accepted identifiers are archived
  under data/raw/recovered_sources/eodhd/ (dre_eodhd_raw.json,
  re_eodhd_raw.json, wrk_eodhd_raw.json) together with the identifier,
  company name, endpoint, parameters and retrieval timestamp. A token-leak
  check is performed before every write.

Outputs
  data/raw/yahoo/dataset_a_adjusted_close_repaired.csv
    798 rows x 101 columns; the 97 Yahoo columns byte-identical to the
    original file (verified programmatically), plus validated EODHD series
    for DRE (694 obs), RE (779 obs) and WRK (779 obs). The original
    dataset_a_adjusted_close.csv is untouched.
  reports/dataset_a_repair_report.md
    Full candidate-by-candidate audit trail.

D. METHODOLOGICAL DECISIONS (rationale)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Official M6 data for validation only, never for history construction
  The official file starts 2022-01-31, so it cannot supply the 2020-2021
  context anyway; more fundamentally, using the evaluation-period source to
  build model inputs would blur the line between data acquisition and the
  competition ground truth. Using it purely as an independent cross-check
  establishes identity of the recovered securities without contaminating the
  input data.

Why provider price levels may differ
  Adjusted closes are back-computed from dividend/split factors relative to a
  reference date; providers snapshot at different times and may round
  differently. Two correct series for the same security can therefore differ
  in level while agreeing in returns.

Why return agreement is the key validation measure
  Returns are invariant to the adjustment anchor, and all downstream research
  quantities (forecasts, quintiles, RPS) are functions of returns, not
  levels. A return correlation of ~1.0 with negligible absolute differences
  identifies the security beyond reasonable doubt; a low correlation (e.g.
  SW.US at 0.297) proves a different economic entity even when the name is
  related.

Why DRE must eventually be constant, never zero
  After the acquisition, DRE shareholders' positions were converted; the
  competition's convention was "no subsequent price change", i.e. zero
  returns. Setting the price to zero would instead imply a -100% return — an
  enormous fictitious crash. Stage 2 will carry the final price forward;
  Stage 1B deliberately leaves post-2022-10-03 dates missing so the raw layer
  records only genuine observations.

Why RE remains the canonical symbol
  The research evaluation period ends 2023-02-03, before the July 2023
  RE -> EG rename. All official M6 artifacts identify the asset as RE, so the
  research keeps that identifier; the provider mapping (EG.US) is recorded as
  provenance metadata instead of leaking into the schema.

Why CARR and OGN are not backfilled
  Their short histories reflect real listing dates (2020 and 2021 spin-offs).
  Inventing pre-listing prices would fabricate data. Later model inputs must
  use the available history (fewer than 512 returns at early origins) rather
  than forcing a fixed-length context.

G. STAGE 2 — PREPROCESSING AND DAILY LOG-RETURN CONSTRUCTION
   (executed 2026-07-17)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Purpose
  Convert the raw repaired Dataset A into (i) a cleaned adjusted-close panel
  on one shared calendar and (ii) a daily log-return panel — the direct
  inputs for the later rolling-origin evaluation. The stage performs exactly
  four substantive transformations (weekday reindexing, post-inception
  forward-filling, the official DRE treatment, log-return calculation) and
  nothing else: no averaging, smoothing, normalisation, standardisation,
  clipping or outlier removal, and no statistics fitted on the data.

Input
  data/raw/yahoo/dataset_a_adjusted_close_repaired.csv (798 rows x 101
  columns, 2020-01-02 .. 2023-02-03). Structural validation before
  processing: `date` first column; exactly 100 asset columns matching the
  official first-occurrence order (names/order confirmed against
  data/raw/m6_official/assets_m6.csv — its prices were never used); unique
  sorted dates with the expected endpoints; no zero or negative prices. The
  input file's SHA-256 hash was recorded before and re-verified after
  processing: the raw file was not modified.

Shared Monday-to-Friday calendar
  A single weekday calendar from 2020-01-02 through 2023-02-03 (807 dates, no
  Saturdays/Sundays) replaces the raw file's union-of-exchanges calendar.
  Rationale: the universe mixes US-listed and London-listed assets whose
  exchanges observe different holidays; cross-sectional work (a return for
  every asset on every evaluation date) requires one common index. Individual
  per-exchange calendars would leave the panels ragged. Reindexing added 9
  dates — weekdays on which no exchange in the universe traded (shared
  holidays such as Good Friday and Christmas-period closures).

Forward-filling (and why it is legitimate)
  For each asset independently, the most recently observed price is carried
  forward across later missing weekdays, starting only AFTER the asset's
  first genuine observation. A missing weekday price means the market was
  closed; a holder's position could not change value that day, so repeating
  the last price — which was genuinely observed earlier, never taken from the
  future, another asset, or the official file — is the faithful
  representation, and it yields exactly zero log return on closure days
  without ever setting a price to zero (log of a zero price would be
  undefined; a zero price would mean a -100% return). Backward-filling and
  interpolation are prohibited because both leak future information.
  Results: 2,851 cells filled in total. Typical US-listed assets: 28 cells
  (US public holidays); London-listed ETFs: 26 cells (UK holidays, longest
  internal gap 2 weekdays, e.g. Christmas + Boxing Day); CARR 26; OGN 16;
  DRE 113 (24 ordinary holiday fills + 89 post-acquisition weekdays, see
  below). No internal gap longer than 5 consecutive weekdays existed
  anywhere before filling.

DRE treatment (official M6 convention)
  Verified: DRE's final genuine observation is 2022-10-03 at 48.20 (positive,
  non-null). That price is carried forward over the 89 subsequent weekdays
  through 2023-02-03, so the processed DRE price is constant after the
  Prologis acquisition and every DRE log return after 2022-10-03 is exactly
  zero — implementing the M6 instruction of "no subsequent price change".
  No PLD prices were used and no jump was introduced at the acquisition date.

CARR and OGN (no backfilling before inception)
  First genuine dates are unchanged by preprocessing: CARR 2020-03-19 and
  OGN 2021-05-14 (Yahoo's series includes OGN's when-issued trading ahead of
  the June-2021 Merck spin-off completion, so its genuine start is earlier
  than the formal distribution date). All weekdays before each first genuine
  observation remain missing: inventing pre-listing prices would fabricate
  data for securities that did not yet trade.

Daily log returns
  Formula: log_return[t] = log(price[t] / price[t-1]), implemented as
  np.log(processed_prices).diff(), per asset, on the shared weekday index in
  official column order. A return is missing exactly where the current or
  preceding processed price is missing (pre-inception periods and the first
  calendar row); missing returns were NOT replaced with zero — a zero return
  asserts "no movement", which is only true for genuine closure days already
  handled by forward-filling. Unchanged prices give exactly 0.0 (the fill is
  an identical float, so the ratio is exactly 1). No infinities exist
  (prices are strictly positive; verified programmatically). Valid returns:
  806 of 807 rows for 98 assets; CARR 751; OGN 450.

Output files
  data/processed/dataset_a_adjusted_close_weekday.csv   807 x 101
  data/processed/dataset_a_daily_log_returns.csv        807 x 101
  data/metadata/dataset_a_preprocessing_summary.csv     per-asset dates/counts
  reports/dataset_a_preprocessing_report.md             full validation report
  No raw file was overwritten (hash-verified).

Validation results
  All checks passed: 807 weekday rows, correct endpoints, no weekends, no
  duplicates, official column order preserved, all 100 assets present,
  forward-filling never touched a leading missing period, DRE constant with
  zero returns after 2022-10-03, no infinite or invalid returns, and all 13
  M6 Friday anchor dates (2022-03-04 .. 2023-02-03, four-week spacing) exist
  in both panels. Focused pytest suite
  (tests/test_preprocess_dataset_a.py): 9 tests, all passing — including
  synthetic-data tests of the fill rule, the log-return formula, the
  missing-not-zero rule and the no-infinity rule, plus integration tests on
  the actual outputs.

Remaining limitations
  - CARR has 511 and OGN has 210 valid returns on or before the first
    forecast origin (2022-03-04) — below the 512-observation context length.
    All other assets have at least 512 (maximum 566). The model-input stage
    must use the available shorter history for these two assets rather than
    padding or fabricating observations.
  - Forward-filled closure days are constant-price observations by
    construction; any later volatility estimates will see these as zero
    returns, which is the intended competition-consistent interpretation.

Explicitly not done in this stage
  No rolling-origin windows, model contexts, forecasts, quintile targets or
  RPS calculations were created; no statistical scaling, averaging,
  smoothing, clipping or outlier removal occurred anywhere.

H. STAGE 3 — ROLLING-ORIGIN MODEL-CONTEXT CONSTRUCTION
   (executed 2026-07-17)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Purpose of rolling-origin evaluation
  Rather than judging models on a single train/test split, the research
  re-forecasts at every scored M6 round: at each forecast origin the model
  sees only history up to that date and predicts the following evaluation
  period. Repeating this across 12 consecutive rounds produces an
  out-of-sample evaluation that mirrors exactly what a live M6 participant
  faced, and prevents any single lucky or unlucky period from dominating
  conclusions.

Round schedule
  13 consecutive Friday anchors, four weeks apart, define 12 rounds:
  2022-03-04, 2022-04-01, 2022-04-29, 2022-05-27, 2022-06-24, 2022-07-22,
  2022-08-19, 2022-09-16, 2022-10-14, 2022-11-11, 2022-12-09, 2023-01-06,
  2023-02-03. For round r, anchor r is the forecast origin (context end) and
  anchor r+1 is the evaluation end; each round's evaluation end is the next
  round's origin (verified). Submission deadlines were the Sundays two days
  after each origin Friday, which is why the origin-date return is
  legitimately part of the model input: the Friday close was public before
  the submission deadline.

Context versus forecast dates
  Context dates (512 shared weekdays ending on and including the origin) are
  what a model may see. Forecast dates (the following 20 shared weekdays,
  ending on the next anchor) are what is being predicted and are entirely
  excluded from model input — including any of them would leak the future.
  Both ranges for every round were validated against the pre-specified
  official table and match exactly (round 1 context 2020-03-19..2022-03-04,
  forecast 2022-03-07..2022-04-01; ... ; round 12 context
  2021-01-21..2023-01-06, forecast 2023-01-09..2023-02-03).

Context movement and overlap
  Each origin lies exactly 20 weekday rows after the previous one, so each
  new context drops its oldest 20 dates and appends the 20 newest observed
  dates; consecutive contexts share 492 of 512 dates (verified). Contexts
  overlap because they are histories; the 12 evaluation periods are strictly
  disjoint (verified), so no realised outcome is ever scored twice.

Fixed 512-weekday context and the missing-history rule
  512 refers to 512 shared weekday time positions, not 512 non-null values
  per asset. Per-asset windows were never extended backwards to compensate
  for missing values, no leading gaps were filled or zero-padded, and no
  asset or round was discarded. CARR has 511 valid returns in round 1 (its
  first context date is its first price date, whose return is undefined) and
  the full 512 from round 2 onward. OGN, listed May 2021, is short in every

K. STAGE 5 — M6 QUINTILE POST-PROCESSING AND RPS EVALUATION
   (executed locally 2026-08-06)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why post-processing is required
  M6 does not score return forecasts. It scores, for each asset, a probability
  distribution over five cross-sectional quintiles of the four-week return, and
  it scores them with the Ranked Probability Score. Chronos produces sampled
  future log-return paths, so a defined conversion is needed from raw sample
  paths to M6-format probabilities. Keeping the raw samples (Stage 4) and doing
  the conversion here means the conversion is documented, testable and
  revisable.

Raw input format
  One NPZ per model per round: forecast_samples (100 assets, 100 sampled
  trajectories, 20 forecast weekdays), asset_symbols, forecast_dates. The NPZ
  files are read-only research artifacts; the evaluator hashes all 24 before and
  after each run and aborts if a single byte changed.

Sampled-return conversion
  For one asset and one sampled trajectory with daily log returns r_1..r_20:
    L = sum(r_h)        (four-week log return)
    R = exp(L) - 1      (four-week simple return)
  applied to all 100 assets, all 100 samples, all 12 rounds, giving 100 x 100
  sampled four-week returns per round.

Cross-sectional ranking
  The M6 task is cross-sectional, so each sampled future is treated as one
  complete scenario for the whole universe: within sample s the 100 assets are
  ranked from lowest to highest four-week return and split into five equal
  quintiles (lowest 20% = Rank1 … highest 20% = Rank5). This is repeated for all
  100 sampled futures.

Probability construction
  An asset's probability for a quintile is its average membership of that
  quintile across the 100 sampled futures (e.g. membership counts 8/17/29/31/15
  give [0.08, 0.17, 0.29, 0.31, 0.15]). Every row is checked to sum to 1
  (observed max deviation 2.2e-16) and to lie within [0, 1].

Tie handling (official principle, with two documented departures)
  Ranking uses rank(method="min"); a block of k tied assets occupies k
  consecutive rank slots and each tied asset receives the AVERAGE quintile
  membership of those slots — the official "Handle Ties" principle from
  RPS and IR calculation.py. Untied assets therefore receive one-hot targets
  such as [0,0,0,1,0], and fractional membership is retained when a tied block
  crosses a quintile boundary. The same function is used for realised outcomes
  and for sampled forecasts; applying it to sampled forecasts is this project's
  post-processing extension, since the official script defines the treatment
  only for realised outcomes. It matters because Chronos output is quantised
  and can repeat values.
  Departure 1: quintile boundaries are computed from the number of ranked
  assets (100). The official script uses total_ranks = max(min-rank), which
  falls below 100 whenever ties exist and would shift every boundary. The two
  definitions agree exactly when there are no ties.
  Departure 2: the official if/elif chain assigns membership only for the FIRST
  quintile a tied block touches, so a boundary-straddling block yields a row
  summing to less than 1. Here membership is spread over every quintile the
  block spans, so all rows sum to 1, as the M6 probability format requires.
  A faithful port of the official tie block is kept in the evaluator solely to
  measure agreement. In practice the realised M6 returns contain NO ties in any
  of the 12 rounds, so all 1,200 ground-truth rows match the official port
  exactly and neither departure affects any reported ground-truth value.

Ground truth (built once, reused by both models)
  Source: Data/assets_m6.csv — the official M6 daily adjusted closes (100
  symbols, 2022-01-31 to 2023-02-17). The Stage 2/3 Yahoo panel is NOT used to
  define outcomes. For round r the interval is anchor r (the forecast origin) to
  anchor r+1, exactly the interval the model forecast, and
    actual_return = (close_price - open_price) / open_price
  matching the official evaluator. Missing prices follow the official rule —
  the most recent available price at or before the date is carried forward —
  applied over the full price history rather than only within the round window,
  because DRE stops appearing in the file after 2022-11-28 and the official
  in-window lookup would have nothing to fall back on for the last rounds. No
  other interpolation is used. The realised returns are then ranked
  cross-sectionally and converted to Rank1..Rank5 with the same tie-aware
  function. All 13 anchors are present in the price file, and the resulting DRE
  return is exactly 0.0 in rounds 9-12, as the competition treatment requires.

RPS
  Reference: RPS Reference/RPS and IR calculation.py (Mcompetitions/M6-methods).
  Only the RPS methodology was extracted and reimplemented; the file is not
  executed or copied wholesale. For one asset:
    RPS = mean( (cumsum(actual) - cumsum(forecast))^2 )
  over the five cumulative positions. Aggregation: one RPS per asset per round
  (1,200 per model), the round score is the mean of its 100 asset scores, and
  the final score is the mean of the 12 round scores. Because every round holds
  exactly 100 assets this equals the mean of all 1,200 values, which the
  evaluator verifies (agreement to 0.0). Lower is better.

Information ratio — excluded
  IR is outside this research. IR_calculation() is not called, ported or
  reproduced, and no investment weights, portfolio returns, return standard
  deviations, information ratios or overall M6 competition rankings are
  computed anywhere in Stage 5.

Sanity checks performed (all passed)

  12 rounds x 100 assets for every model; NPZ shapes (100, 100, 20) and all
  values finite; predicted and actual asset universes identical; forecast dates
  equal to the scheduled round windows; no missing RPS values; every round mean
  equals the mean of its 100 asset scores; every final mean equals the mean of
  the 12 round means and of the 1,200 asset scores; all 24 raw NPZ files

Why the two models differ (diagnostic, not a result)
  RPS penalises confident-and-wrong more heavily than diffuse. The evaluator
  records, per round, the average largest quintile probability and a dispersion
  ratio = (spread of the assets' median predicted four-week returns) / (typical
  spread within one asset's own samples). Chronos Base: mean max probability
  0.529, dispersion ratio 1.97 — its cross-sectional ordering barely changes
  between sampled futures, so it issues near-one-hot bets (39 of its 1,200 rows
  are exactly one-hot) and is punished when they are wrong. Financial Chronos:
  mean max probability 0.396, dispersion ratio 0.57 — sampling noise reshuffles
  the ordering, so its probabilities stay diffuse and closer to the benchmark.
  The Base model's low RPS is therefore an over-confidence effect, not evidence
  of a better ranking signal.

Exploratory test 1 — DRE zero-adjustment (2026-08-06)
  Testing_What_Works/ is a new top-level folder, separate from Results/, for
  exploratory "what improves the RPS?" checks. It holds no primary results.
  First test: raw DRE forecasts versus DRE set to zero in rounds 9-12 (round 8
  deliberately unchanged), reusing the Stage 5 evaluator and the derived copies
  already in Results/Evaluation/derived_dre_adjusted/. Rounds 1-8 are identical
  in both variants (verified, difference 0.0) and no raw NPZ was touched.
  Outcome: the adjustment does NOT improve RPS — both models get very slightly
  worse. Chronos Base 0.226899 -> 0.227017 (+0.000118); Financial Chronos
  0.179368 -> 0.179398 (+0.000029). The model ranking is unchanged.
  Reason: M6 quintiles are cross-sectional, so a 0% forecast is ranked against
  the other 99 assets' FORECASTS, not against zero. In rounds 9-12 Chronos Base
  gives 79-92% of the other assets a negative predicted median, so a flat 0%
  DRE ranks near the top of the predicted cross-section, whereas DRE's realised
  0% ranked Q1/Q3/Q3/Q2 in the realised cross-section. Zeroing therefore does
  not move DRE to a better quintile, it only removes forecast uncertainty and
  sharpens an incorrect call (round 9, Chronos Base: [0.04,0.04,0.13,0.63,0.16]
  RPS 0.484 -> [0,0,0,0.87,0.13] RPS 0.603, actual Q1). The effect is tiny
  everywhere (<=0.0014 on any round) because it is one asset of 100 in four
  rounds of twelve.
  Decision: keep the raw forecasts as the primary evaluation; the M6 rule fixes
  the REALISED return at zero, not the forecast. Revisit only if Round 8's
  date-specific treatment is defined, or as a footnote showing that the DRE
  handling drives no conclusion.
  Artifacts: Testing_What_Works/dre_zero_test_results.csv and
  Testing_What_Works/dre_zero_test_summary.md.

M. CHRONOS-2 POST-PROCESSING METHOD 1 — SPATIOTEMPORAL GAUSSIAN COPULA
   (executed locally 2026-08-07)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why post-processing is required
  Chronos-2 outputs MARGINAL quantiles: for each asset and each of the 20
  forecast days it gives 21 quantiles of that day's log return, and nothing
  about how the days or the assets move together. The M6 task needs the
  opposite: a four-week return (a sum ACROSS the 20 days) ranked CROSS-
  SECTIONALLY (a comparison ACROSS the 100 assets). Both of those operations
  depend on joint structure the marginals do not contain. The two earlier models
  produced sampled trajectories, which carry that structure implicitly; for
  Chronos-2 it has to be supplied explicitly. This is a methodological addition
  on top of the model, and results must be attributed to the model AND the
  chosen coupling, not to the model alone.

Method chosen (the first of possibly several)
  A separable spatiotemporal Gaussian copula. Chronos-2 supplies the marginals;
  all dependence comes from the round's own history:
    latent Z ~ N(0, C_time (x) C_asset)     (Kronecker product => separable)
    U = Phi(Z), clipped to [0.01, 0.99]
    scenario return = Chronos-2 marginal quantile curve evaluated at U
  Implemented in scripts/evaluate_chronos2_spatiotemporal_copula.py (CPU only,
  no GPU and no model reload).

Marginal curves
  Each (asset, day) curve is a monotonic piecewise-linear interpolation of the
  21 native quantiles, so any percentile can be requested. Percentiles outside
  0.01-0.99 are CLIPPED to those boundaries rather than extrapolated: no tail
  behaviour beyond what the model actually reported is invented. A cumulative
  maximum guards against numerical quantile crossing (none was present in the
  raw files). The raw NPZs are opened read-only and were unchanged.

Cross-asset dependence (100x100), from that round's context only
  Each asset's observed 512-day log returns are converted to empirical
  percentile ranks and then to standard-normal scores. Rows are restricted to
  dates where EVERY asset has a genuine observation: leading missing history is
  never backfilled, zero-filled or imputed, so those rows are simply not usable
  for a joint estimate. Usable rows therefore rise with OGN's shrinking gap:
  210 in round 1 (OGN 302 missing, CARR 1) up to 430 in round 12 (OGN 82). The
  correlation is estimated with LEDOIT-WOLF shrinkage (sklearn LedoitWolf),
  converted from covariance to correlation, symmetrised, and checked for
  positive definiteness. Realised shrinkage fell from 0.0432 (round 1, fewest
  observations) to 0.0153 (round 12) - small, as expected once n > p. The
  minimum eigenvalue was comfortably positive in all 12 rounds (0.047 down to
  0.017), so the eigenvalue-repair path was never needed.

Temporal dependence (20x20)
  A single pooled lag-one parameter is estimated across assets on the Gaussian
  score series and used to build corr(i,j) = rho^|i-j|. The estimates are
  essentially zero in every round: rho ranged from -0.0179 (round 1) to +0.0137
  (round 9), median about +0.005. It was deliberately NOT floored away from
  zero, so the time correlation matrix is very close to the identity. In
  practice this method is therefore almost purely SPATIAL: daily M6 log returns
  show no usable lag-one structure, and the "temporal" half of the copula
  contributes almost nothing. That is a finding, not a defect.

Scenario generation
  Z = L_asset @ G @ L_time' with G iid standard normal gives unit marginal
  variances and the intended Kronecker covariance. 1,000 scenarios per round,
  numpy default_rng seeded with 42 at the start of every round, so each round is
  independently reproducible. Output shape (100 assets, 1000 scenarios, 20 days).
  These are DERIVED COPULA SCENARIOS, NOT NATIVE CHRONOS-2 SAMPLES: only the
  marginals come from the model, and every cross-asset and cross-day relation is
  imposed here from history. They must never be described as model samples.

Conversion to M6 probabilities and RPS — existing methodology reused
  The (100, 1000, 20) scenario array is passed straight into the primary
  evaluator's own functions (scripts/evaluate_m6_rps.py): four-week return
  exp(sum of 20 log returns) - 1, cross-sectional ranking of all 100 assets per
  scenario, the official tie-aware quintile rule, averaging membership across
  scenarios, and the official RPS against the same assets_m6.csv ground truth.
  No separate scorer was written. Verified per round: probability rows sum to 1
  (max deviation 2e-16) and lie in [0, 1]; each quintile receives exactly 20
  units of cross-sectional mass; asset ordering preserved; 1,200 asset-round
  scores; mean of the 12 round means equals the mean of the 1,200 scores.

    comparable with the two sampled-trajectory models as a pure model
    comparison, and a different copula could change it.

    effectively spatial; the AR(1) component is close to the identity and is
    carrying almost no information.
  - Dependence uses complete-case rows only, so early rounds rest on fewer
    observations (210 in round 1) purely because OGN listed in May 2021. Round 1
    is also the worst round (0.1953), which is consistent with, though not proof
    of, a weaker dependence estimate there.
  - A Gaussian copula has no tail dependence: joint extreme moves across assets
    are under-represented relative to real markets.
  - Dependence is assumed constant over the 20-day horizon and estimated from
    the trailing 512 days, so it cannot react to a regime change inside the
    forecast window.
  - 1,000 scenarios resolve each probability to 0.001; Monte Carlo error is
    small relative to the differences reported here but not zero.

N. CHRONOS-2 POST-PROCESSING METHOD 2 — INDEPENDENT QUANTILE SAMPLING
   (executed locally 2026-08-07)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why this was tested
  Method 1 (section M) scored 0.165418 by wrapping Chronos-2's marginal
  quantiles in a spatiotemporal Gaussian copula. That result confounds two
  things: the quality of Chronos-2's marginals, and the value of the dependence
  structure imposed on top of them. This experiment is the ABLATION that
  separates them. It is a comparison/sensitivity method, not a competing
  headline result: it exists to answer whether the dependence modelling
  materially improved Chronos-2's M6 performance.

The single change
  Every percentile is sampled INDEPENDENTLY for every (asset, scenario,
  forecast day). There is no cross-asset correlation matrix, no Ledoit-Wolf
  estimation, no temporal rho, no AR(1) time matrix and no Gaussian copula; no
  historical dependence is fitted at all, so the round's Stage 3 context is not
  even read. This is the simplest possible quantile-to-scenario conversion.

Everything else held identical (verified, not assumed)
  Same 12 raw Chronos-2 NPZs and the same validation; the same monotone
  piecewise-linear interpolation of the 21 native quantiles; the same clipping
  of percentiles to 0.01-0.99 with no invented tails; 1,000 scenarios; seed 42;
  the same four-week aggregation exp(sum of 20 log returns) - 1; the same
  cross-sectional ranking, tie handling, ground truth and RPS scorer. The
  script IMPORTS those functions from the method-1 script and from
  scripts/evaluate_m6_rps.py rather than copying them, and asserts at runtime
  that N_SCENARIOS and RANDOM_SEED match method 1, so the two experiments
  cannot silently drift apart. A test also asserts the two modules share the
  same interpolation and loading functions by identity. No difference other
  than the percentile dependence mechanism was found.

Nature of the generated data
  The (100 assets, 1000 scenarios, 20 days) array holds INDEPENDENTLY SAMPLED
  SCENARIOS DERIVED FROM CHRONOS-2 MARGINAL QUANTILE FORECASTS. They are NOT
  native Chronos-2 trajectories: only the marginals come from the model, and
  here nothing whatsoever links one asset, day or scenario to another.

Did dependence modelling materially help? No.
  The entire spatiotemporal copula apparatus - complete-case Gaussian scores,
  Ledoit-Wolf shrunk 100x100 correlation, AR(1) time matrix, Kronecker copula -
  bought 0.000141 of overall RPS, about 0.09% relative, and it does not win a
  majority of rounds by a convincing margin (7 of 12). On this evidence
  Chronos-2's M6 performance comes from the quality of its MARGINAL quantile
  forecasts, not from the joint structure imposed afterwards.
  Two mechanisms explain this, and both were visible in method 1:
  (a) the estimated temporal dependence was already ~0 (rho between -0.018 and
      +0.014), so the copula's time component was effectively the identity and
      only its cross-asset component could ever have mattered;
  (b) M6 scores a CROSS-SECTIONAL ranking, and positive cross-asset correlation
      largely acts as a common market factor that shifts all 100 assets
      together - ranking removes exactly that component, so it cannot change
      the quintile assignment much.
  Important nuance: the two methods do NOT produce the same forecasts. Comparing
  the 1,200 asset-round probability vectors gives a mean absolute difference of
  0.0395 per cell, a mean total-variation distance of 0.099, a correlation of
  0.763, and the same modal quintile in only 718 of 1,200 cases. Sharpness is
  almost identical (mean maximum probability 0.3117 copula vs 0.3127
  independent). So the dependence structure meaningfully RESHUFFLES which asset
  receives which probability, but does not make those probabilities any better.
  That is a stronger statement than "the methods agree", and it is the more
  useful finding for the write-up.

Limitations
  - This is an ablation of one coupling choice, not proof that dependence is
    irrelevant in general: a copula with tail dependence, or a non-separable or
    time-varying structure, could behave differently.
  - 1,000 scenarios resolve each probability to 0.001, and the copula-versus-
    independent gap (0.000141) is smaller than the Monte Carlo noise one would
    expect from a different seed, so the two should be read as TIED rather than
    as the copula being narrowly ahead.
  - Both variants still fail to beat the naive 0.160000 benchmark overall.
