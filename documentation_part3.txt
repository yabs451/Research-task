--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. PURPOSE OF THE HELD-OUT EVALUATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Section P of Part 2 set out the question this stage exists to answer. Every
time-series foundation model tested in Stages 1-5 was beaten by a forecast that
simply assigns 0.2 to each quintile:

    Naive equal-probability benchmark        0.160000
    Chronos-2 120M + spatiotemporal copula   0.165418
    Chronos-2 120M + independent sampling    0.165559
    Financial Chronos Small 46M              0.179368
    Chronos T5 Base 200M                     0.226899

On its own that cannot distinguish two explanations:
  (a) the M6 ranking task is close to unpredictable at this horizon, so nothing
      much beats the uniform forecast - a statement about the TASK; or
  (b) the TSFMs specifically are poorly suited to it, while conventional
      feature-based learners do better - a statement about the MODELS.

A competition-grade supervised baseline built for exactly this ranking task
supplies the missing reference point. This stage produces it, on the same 12
official M6 rounds, scored by the same evaluator, against the same ground truth.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. THE 12 M6 ORIGINS AND THE EVALUATION PROCEDURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Origins (forecast made at the Friday origin; the scored four-week period runs to
the following grid Friday):

   R1  2022-03-04 -> 2022-04-01      R7  2022-08-19 -> 2022-09-16
   R2  2022-04-01 -> 2022-04-29      R8  2022-09-16 -> 2022-10-14
   R3  2022-04-29 -> 2022-05-27      R9  2022-10-14 -> 2022-11-11
   R4  2022-05-27 -> 2022-06-24      R10 2022-11-11 -> 2022-12-09
   R5  2022-06-24 -> 2022-07-22      R11 2022-12-09 -> 2023-01-06
   R6  2022-07-22 -> 2022-08-19      R12 2023-01-06 -> 2023-02-03

  These are the project's existing official rounds. The script cross-checks the
  M6 rows of `feature_baseline_origin_schedule.csv` against
  `Data/metadata/m6_round_schedule.csv` and refuses to run if the origins or the
  target ends disagree.

Causal expanding retraining
  At each origin T the training set is every row of that branch with
  `eligible_for_training_from <= T`, i.e. whose own four-week outcome had
  already been realised at T - exactly the rule used during tuning. The model is
  REFITTED FROM SCRATCH at each of the 12 origins on the expanded history:

      Round  1  (2022-03-04)  14,163 training rows
      Round 12  (2023-01-06)  15,263 training rows

  Earlier M6 rounds therefore become training rows for later rounds, which is
  causally sound because by then their four-week outcome had occurred. Note
  those rows carry this project's DATASET-D historical labels, never the
  official M6 outcome; the official outcome never enters training in any form.

  VERIFIED: across both branches and all 12 rounds, no training row had
  `target_end_date > T`, no training row had `origin_date >= T`, and every round
  predicted exactly 100 assets. At round 12 the latest training origin is
  2022-12-09 with target end 2023-01-06 - the boundary, not beyond it.

Probability post-processing and the validity rule
  The released Samartzis cross-sectional adjustment is applied unchanged:

      adjusted = raw + (0.2 - column_mean)

  Each quintile column is shifted until its cross-sectional mean is 0.20; row
  sums are preserved exactly. A forecast is valid when every entry lies in
  [0, 1] and every row sums to 1 within 1e-9.

  If a configuration produces an invalid adjusted probability at ANY round it is
  STOPPED. Probabilities are never clipped or renormalised, no alternative
  hyperparameter set is substituted, nothing is retuned, and no mean M6 RPS is
  reported for it - a partial mean would misrepresent a model that cannot
  produce valid forecasts across the held-out period. The other configurations
  continue unaffected. This is the same infeasibility criterion used during
  tuning (Part 2, section AS), applied here to a frozen model rather than to a
  candidate.

Scoring
  The official ground truth is loaded only AFTER every forecast for a
  configuration has been produced. It is built by the project's existing
  `build_ground_truth(load_prices(), schedule)` from `Data/assets_m6.csv` and
  scored with the project's existing `rps_scores`, both imported from
  `scripts/evaluate_m6_rps.py`, which was not modified. Forecasts are aligned to
  the ground truth's own per-round symbol order before scoring, so a mis-ordered
  prediction cannot silently score against the wrong asset.

  benchmark on the held-out M6 sample, while every TSFM tested in Stages 1-5 did
  not. That is evidence for explanation (b) of section A: the M6 ranking task is
  not simply unpredictable at this horizon - a purpose-built feature-based
  learner extracts a small but consistent edge from it - whereas zero-shot
  time-series foundation models underperform even the uniform forecast.

  The edge is genuine but SMALL: about 0.0029 RPS, roughly 1.8% relative to the
  naive benchmark, spread across 9 of 12 rounds rather than concentrated in one.
  Nothing here should be read as strong forecasting skill.

  wants to see: RF/no_knn 0.155614 -> 0.157053 (+0.001439), RF/knn 0.155624 ->

  optimistically biased; the held-out numbers are not, and they remain below
  0.16.

  The KNN and no-KNN branches are practically indistinguishable (RF: 0.157053 vs
  0.157149, a gap of 0.0001). On this evidence the Samartzis-style KNN treatment
  of missing returns does not materially affect final M6 performance, which is
  the question the two-pipeline design was built to answer.

E. CONFIGURATION FAILURE - LGBM / KNN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Status          STOPPED, no M6 RPS reported
  Failed at       Round 11, origin 2022-12-09
  Reason          adjusted probability below 0 (minimum -0.001070)
  Rounds valid    1-10 produced valid probabilities before the failure

  This is the risk identified and quantified before tuning (Part 2, section AS):
  the released adjustment forces each quintile column to a cross-sectional mean
  of 0.20 but does not bound individual entries, so a sharp cross-section can
  push a tail entry past a bound. The diagnostic found this to be marginal for
  Random Forest but substantial in parts of the LightGBM space, and the
  infeasibility rule was adopted precisely so that tuning would not select such
  a configuration. LGBM/knn passed all 12 TUNING origins, so it was selectable;
  it then failed on one held-out round.

  The violation is small in magnitude (-0.001070, one entry). It was NOT
  clipped, renormalised, rounded away or excused, and no substitute
  hyperparameter set was tried, because the instruction for this stage is
  explicit: a configuration that cannot produce valid forecasts across the
  held-out M6 period must not be given a misleading complete M6 RPS. Its
  rounds 1-10 forecasts are also excluded from the predictions file, so no
  partial result can be mistaken for a complete one.

  What this means for the research question: three of the four configurations
  completed, including one LightGBM variant, so the comparison in section D is
  not compromised. The honest statement is that the released post-processing
  rule is not robust for LightGBM on this data, not that LightGBM/KNN is a poor
  forecaster - its rounds 1-10 were valid and it was competitive in tuning.
  Whether to revisit the post-processing for LightGBM is a decision for a later
  stage, not something this stage was permitted to resolve.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
H. THE FEATURE-BASED MODEL FAMILY - ACADEMIC JUSTIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The three feature-based classifiers in this project form an M6 benchmark adapted
from the feature-based classification methodology of Samartzis (2025). They are
NOT an exact reproduction of that work: the predictors, the future four-week
quintile target, the forecast-origin alignment, the causal training rules and
the two preprocessing branches are this project's own finalized decisions
(documentation_part2.txt, sections AD-AT), while the model families, their
hyperparameter search spaces and the cross-sectional probability
post-processing are preserved from the source.

The three were chosen to span distinct hypothesis classes rather than to
accumulate models:

  RANDOM FOREST
    A nonlinear bagging-based tree ensemble. It provides a robust feature-based
    classifier and tests whether nonlinear interactions between financial
    predictors contribute useful relative-performance information.

  LIGHTGBM
    A nonlinear gradient-boosted tree ensemble. Its inclusion provides a
    distinct ensemble-learning mechanism from Random Forest and enables
    comparison of boosting against bagging.

  SOFTMAX REGRESSION
    A low-complexity and interpretable linear multiclass classifier that
    directly produces five-quintile probability forecasts. It tests whether the
    predictive signal captured by the feature-based models requires nonlinear
    tree ensembles or can largely be represented by a much simpler linear
    decision structure.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. STAGE 9 - SOFTMAX REGRESSION: TUNING AND HELD-OUT M6 EVALUATION
   (executed 2026-08-21)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why Softmax, and why now
  Stage 8 established that the tree ensembles beat the naive benchmark by about
  0.003 RPS. That leaves open whether the edge comes from nonlinear structure or
  from a much simpler linear relationship between the ten predictors and the
  cross-sectional quintile. Softmax answers that directly: same predictors, same
  target, same origins, same causal rules, same probability post-processing -
  only the decision structure changes, from tree ensembles to a linear
  multiclass boundary.

Input branch
  The finalized KNN supervised dataset, `supervised_rows_knn.csv`, used
  READ-ONLY. It was chosen because 8 of its 10 predictors are already complete,
  so the amount of extra handling Softmax needs is minimal.

Residual missing values, and why imputation was required here
  In the KNN branch the only remaining NaNs are in the two volume-derived
  features - they arise from the Samartzis zero-volume rule (section AR of
  Part 2) and are irreducible by KNN, which only ever touched the return matrix:

    feature                        NaN cells   affected rows
    feat_dollar_volume_2m              382     -
    feat_abs_ret_to_volume_3m          521     -
    other eight predictors               0     -
    TOTAL                              903     493 of 14,163 historical (3.48%)
                                               28 of 1,200 M6 rows   (2.33%)

  Random Forest and LightGBM consume these natively. scikit-learn's
  LogisticRegression cannot: it raises `ValueError: Input X contains NaN` at
  fit time, in this version and for both branches. Softmax therefore requires a
  missing-value decision that the tree models did not, and it cannot be avoided
  by branch choice - the affected assets (SEGA.L, IEFM.L, HIGH.L, DRE) appear in
  all 12 M6 rounds and in 28 rows that must be forecast.

Causal training-median imputation (Softmax only)
  At every origin T, in this order:
    1. select the causally eligible training rows (`eligible_for_training_from
       <= T`, the same rule as RF/LGBM);
    2. compute the median of each of the ten predictors using ONLY those
       training rows;
    3. fill missing values in BOTH the training rows and the prediction rows at
       T with those TRAINING medians.
  Justification: the median is the minimal, distribution-free choice for a
  handful of missing cells; it is refitted at every origin so it uses no future
  information; and applying the training median to the prediction rows keeps the
  prediction block strictly out of the fitted statistics. Medians are never
  computed globally. If a required training median cannot be formed the run
  stops rather than inventing another rule (implemented and unit-tested; it did
  not trigger).
  In practice only the two volume features ever required filling, at every
  origin, exactly as anticipated: 813-903 cells per origin, 10,494 cells in
  total across the 12 M6 rounds.

Causal standardization
  The Samartzis paper states that scale-sensitive models are normalized by
  subtracting the training mean and dividing by the training standard deviation.
  The released `logistic_model` does not itself show such a scaler - the only
  StandardScaler in all_models.py belongs to `mlp_model` - so for this
  scale-sensitive model the project follows the PAPER's stated methodology
  rather than the released code, and records this as a deliberate deviation.
  At every origin a StandardScaler is fitted on the imputed TRAINING predictors
  only, then used to transform both the training and the prediction blocks. It
  is never fitted on prediction rows or future data.

  Full pipeline order, refitted from scratch at every origin:
    causal training selection -> median imputation -> standardization
    -> Softmax fit -> prediction -> 0.20 adjustment -> validation.

  VERIFIED at two origins by independent re-derivation: training blocks are
  centred (|mean| <= 2.8e-16) and unit-scaled (|sd-1| <= 1.1e-16); prediction
  blocks are NOT centred on themselves (|mean| ~ 0.46), proving the scaler was
  fitted on training rows alone; training medians differ from a global median on
  6 of 10 features, proving they are not global; and corrupting every row dated
  after T leaves both design matrices bit-identical.

Nothing was persisted
  The finalized KNN CSV was not modified and no median-filled copy of it exists.
  Imputation and scaling happen inside the Softmax pipeline at each origin and
  are discarded afterwards. Confirmed by SHA-256: `supervised_rows_knn.csv` is
  byte-identical before and after this stage.

Model
  `sklearn.linear_model.LogisticRegression(random_state=42, C=<tuned>,
  max_iter=100)` - the released configuration plus the paper's scaler. Default
  lbfgs solver, multinomial for a 5-class target. Ten predictors, no AssetClass,
  no change to any feature or target definition. Probabilities come directly
  from `predict_proba`, mapped onto the fixed quintile columns (column j =
  quintile j = Rank j+1, quintile 0 = worst realised return). Convergence was
  comfortable throughout: at most 24 lbfgs iterations against a limit of 100.

Probability post-processing
  The released cross-sectional adjustment, unchanged: `adjusted = raw +
  (0.2 - column_mean)`. No clipping, no renormalisation. Validity requires every
  entry in [0, 1] and every row summing to 1 within 1e-9, and the same
  infeasible-candidate principle established for RF/LGBM applies.

Tuning
  Search space, confirmed directly against the released registry before running:
  `'C': hp.uniform('C', 0.5, 1.0)` - a single tuned hyperparameter. The released
  rule MAX_EVALS = 20 x |space| therefore gives exactly 20 TPE evaluations. The
  same 12 pre-M6 tuning origins as RF/LGBM (2021-03-05 .. 2022-01-07), the same
  expanding causal training history (12,866 -> 13,963 rows), seed 42, objective
  = mean RPS over the 12 origins against the KNN branch's own historical target.

Interpretation
  Softmax lands squarely inside the tree-ensemble band: 0.157355 against RF's
  0.157053-0.157149 and LightGBM's 0.157432. The entire spread across the four
  completed feature-based models is 0.00038 RPS, an order of magnitude smaller
  than their common ~0.0029 margin over the naive benchmark.

  The natural reading is that the predictive content in these ten features is
  largely LINEAR in the cross-section: a plain multinomial logistic regression
  recovers essentially all of the edge that bagged and boosted tree ensembles
  find, and it beats one of them. The tree ensembles' nonlinear capacity is not
  buying meaningful additional relative-performance information here. That
  strengthens rather than weakens the Stage 8 conclusion - the edge over the
  naive benchmark is small but is not an artifact of one model family's
  inductive bias, since three structurally different learners agree on it.

  Two cautions. The differences between the four models are far too small to
  rank them meaningfully on 12 rounds. And Softmax's advantage in robustness -
  no invalid probabilities, smallest tuning-to-held-out degradation - is a
  property worth noting alongside its accuracy, not a claim that it forecasts
  better.
